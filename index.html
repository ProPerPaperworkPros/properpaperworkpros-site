Most eviction cases in California go through several stages before a judge makes a final ruling. Understanding each stage helps you stay ahead of deadlines and arrive prepared.

1. Notice Period — The landlord serves a 3-Day Notice to Pay or Quit, Notice to Vacate, or other termination notice. This is your first opportunity to respond, pay, or document your position.

2. Unlawful Detainer Filed — If you don't comply with the notice, the landlord files an eviction lawsuit. You are served with a Summons and Complaint. You now have 10 court days to file a written response or risk a default judgment.

3. Answer Filed — Your written response to the court sets out your position and any defenses. This is one of the most important documents in your case — and one we help prepare.

4. Settlement Conference — Before trial, most courts schedule a settlement conference where both sides meet to try to resolve the matter. Arriving organized and prepared makes a significant difference.

5. Trial — If no settlement is reached, the case goes before a judge. Your organized paperwork, declarations, and evidence are what the judge reviews.

6. Judgment / Stay of Execution — If a judgment is entered against you, a Stay of Execution may delay enforcement while you pursue other remedies. We prepare this paperwork as well.
